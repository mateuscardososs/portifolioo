## Overall Impression

The portfolio is clear, mature, and technically credible. It already communicates backend/full stack direction, real experience, and contact paths. The single biggest opportunity is to stop proving competence through volume and instead build a sharper thesis: why Mateus deserves an interview now.

## What's Working

1. The backend/full stack positioning is clear from the first screen and matches the stated audience.
2. The visual system is cohesive: dark base, teal accent, Inter, short radii, compact cards, and reduced-motion support.
3. The featured project has useful depth and can become the page's strongest proof point.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Hero sells the role, not Mateus**

**Why it matters**: The H1 is "Desenvolvedor Backend & Full Stack", while "Mateus Cardoso" is generated through `h1::after`. That weakens personal-brand recall and puts essential identity in CSS instead of semantic HTML.

**Fix**: Put the name in the HTML hero and rewrite the main thesis around APIs, automacao, dados, and sistemas internos. Make the first fold answer "why interview Mateus?" rather than only "what role does he want?"

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable clarify index.html`

**[P1] Cognitive load is too high in the first half**

**Why it matters**: Four hero CTAs, five nav links, 23 stack items, repeated chips, and dense project tags make recruiters and tech leads triage instead of deciding.

**Fix**: Keep one primary CTA and two secondary actions in the hero. Collapse stack into capability groups, reduce chips, and move the strongest proof closer to the top.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable distill index.html`

**[P2] The personal brand is still too generic**

**Why it matters**: The brief explicitly rejects generic portfolio templates. The current execution is polished but familiar: dark SaaS, teal, cards, chips, hover lifts, repeated section grammar.

**Fix**: Turn "Console de Credibilidade" into an actual evidence component: a compact proof panel with project, context, decision, stack, and review link.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable bolder index.html`

**[P2] Secondary projects need more verifiable technical proof**

**Why it matters**: Tech leads will look for decisions, trade-offs, architecture, tests, endpoints, deployment, or review paths. Several cards describe problem/solution but do not make verification easy.

**Fix**: Add one concrete evidence line per project: "decisao tecnica", "risco tratado", "como revisar", or "parte que implementei".

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable clarify index.html`

**[P3] Motion and hover treatments are too uniform**

**Why it matters**: Almost everything reveals, lifts, and receives teal hover treatment. This makes the UI feel generated by a pattern rather than directed by intent.

**Fix**: Reserve strong hover/lift for clickable or high-priority elements. Let informational cards stay calmer.

**Suggested command**: `$impeccable quieter index.html`

## Persona Red Flags

**Recrutador**: The role is clear, but the next action is not. Four hero CTAs split attention, and "Backend / Full Stack em evolucao" may sound less confident than the rest of the page. The large stack section can read as keyword stuffing.

**Tech lead**: The project principal is promising, but secondary cards need more proof of implementation quality. The lead will want architecture, decisions, tests, risks, or review links, not only a clean problem/solution sentence.

**Visitante mobile**: The page is responsive, but the first screen likely becomes a long sequence of title, copy, four buttons, and profile panel before strong proof appears. Chips and cards may produce tiring scrolling.

## Minor Observations
